# Kitchen window garden

*Care notes for a narrow sill, a west-facing window, and three plants that prefer different answers.*

![[Assets/botanical-journal.webp]]

## The room at 08:10

The glass was cool this morning. The fern had dried at the edges, the mint had leaned toward the kettle, and the small pot of thyme had finally stopped looking offended by the move. I watered slowly and left the blinds open for an hour.

> [!quote] Working note
> A plant does not need a speech. It needs the right amount of water, a little light, and somebody who notices when the leaves change.

### Plants on the sill

| Plant | Light | Last check | Next move |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Sword fern | Soft morning light | Fronds dry at the tips | Mist the air, not the crown |
| Mint | Bright indirect light | New stems leaning west | Turn the pot on Thursday |
| Thyme | Direct afternoon light | Soil dry two knuckles down | Water from the saucer |

### Small jobs

### What to watch

The fern wants humidity but hates wet feet. Mint forgives almost everything except a dark corner. Thyme prefers to dry out between drinks, which feels rude until you see how quickly it recovers.

> [!tip] Pressing notes
> If a leaf falls cleanly, keep it between two sheets of paper. Write the date on the back before you forget which week it belonged to.

#### Evening check

The room looks different when the plants cast shadows across the counter. Nothing has changed much. That is part of the pleasure.
